Mac volume at 100% and still too quiet?

Turn your Mac up past 100%

Subele boosts videos, music and calls up to 400% without them sounding blown out. No audio driver, no account, and it's free.

macOS 14.2 or later · Intel and Apple Silicon · open source (MIT)

When it helps

  • A video or course recorded too quietly to follow, even at full volume.
  • A Zoom, Meet or Teams call where the other person speaks softly.
  • MacBook speakers that fall short in a noisy room.
  • A podcast or film with quiet dialogue and loud explosions.

What you get

Up to 400%

A slider from 50 to 400%. A limiter brings peaks down before they arrive, so the sound holds together as you go up.

Music or Voice

Music turns everything up evenly and keeps the dynamics. Voice lifts what is too quiet, for calls, podcasts and videos with weak audio.

Graphical equalizer

Bass, mids and treble in five bands you drag on the curve, with a live spectrum and presets such as Clear voice or More bass.

No drivers

Built on the audio API in macOS 14.2. No virtual device, and your output stays as it is. To uninstall, delete the app.

Louder without sounding blown out

There are two ways to keep boosted audio from going past full scale. One is to clip or round off every peak that goes over: simple, but it bends the waveform, and that is the tinny "boosted" sound. The other is to see the peak a few milliseconds early and turn the volume down by just enough. Subele does the second.

Total harmonic distortion of a 1 kHz sine with the volume at 400%. In blue, a per-sample soft limiter, computed with the formula FineTune publishes in its source; in amber, Subele, measured with its real processor (it is a test in the repository). It is a test signal. With music the gap changes, and recording each app's actual output is still to be done (to be confirmed). The full test is in the repo.

The limiter also watches the peaks between samples, the ones your Mac's converter can rebuild above full scale even when every sample is below it.

How to install

  1. Download Subele.dmg, open it and drag Subele to Applications.
  2. Open Subele. The first time, macOS will block it; how to open it is below.
  3. When asked, allow Audio Recording. That is what lets it read the system sound to turn it up.
  4. Find the icon in the menu bar and move the slider.

Why macOS blocks it

The app is signed but not notarised: notarisation requires a paid Apple Developer account. You will see "Apple could not verify this app is free of malware". To open it anyway:

System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll to the notice about Subele and click Open Anyway. Or in Terminal:

xattr -d com.apple.quarantine "/Applications/Subele.app"

On macOS 15 the old Control-click → Open trick is no longer enough.

Check the .dmg

Each version publishes the SHA-256 of its files in SHA256SUMS.txt, on the release page. To compare it with your download:

shasum -a 256 ~/Downloads/Subele.dmg

And if you would rather not trust any binary, build it yourself. No Xcode needed:

git clone https://github.com/carlostapiacl/subele
cd subele
./build-app.sh

Questions

Why does it ask for audio recording permission?

To turn the volume up it has to read the sound leaving your Mac, process it and send it back. That permission is how macOS allows it. The audio never leaves your Mac. The app has no server, no account and no telemetry, and the code is public.

Does it work with Bluetooth headphones or speakers?

Yes. It works on whatever output macOS is using and rebuilds itself when you switch. AirPlay speakers are not tested yet (to be confirmed).

Does it add delay?

About 15 ms with the default setting, measured from the audio itself. You won't notice it in music or calls. If you notice it in video, the Latency menu has a 128-frame option of about 9 ms.

Does it drain the battery?

Very little. On a 2019 Intel MacBook Pro with music playing it used about 1% CPU. Apple Silicon should be lower (to be confirmed).

Does it work with eqMac or Boom 3D running?

No. Those apps install their own driver and become the system output, and Subele hangs trying to read it. Quit them before opening Subele.

How do I uninstall it?

Quit it from its menu and delete Subele from Applications. It leaves no driver or anything else installed.

How it works inside

Since macOS 14.2 there is a public API, AudioHardwareCreateProcessTap, that captures what other apps are playing without a driver in between. Subele captures that audio, runs it through gain, an equalizer, a compressor (in Voice mode) and a limiter that looks 3 ms ahead, and plays it back to the same speakers. Your default output does not change.

The details, measurements and tests are in the repository README.
